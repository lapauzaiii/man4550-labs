The Analyst Challenge: AI Decision Model Audits

You are acting as a Business Analytics Associate for Cowford Advisory Group. Your role is to verify quantitative reports before they are trusted for leadership decisions.

Submission Reminder

Submit your completed audit in Canvas.

Overview

Your Role

Professional Context

Throughout the semester, executives from across Cowford Holdings will send quantitative analyses before they are presented to leadership. Some reports contain mathematical mistakes, some contain incorrect assumptions, and some produce the right calculations but the wrong business recommendation.

The Analyst's Rule

"The math checks out" is not verification. Your job is to determine whether the model, assumptions, interpretation, and recommendation are appropriate for the business decision.

What You Are Actually Doing

You Are Auditing a Decision, Not Just Checking a Spreadsheet

In each audit, you will receive an executive-style report that makes a recommendation based on a quantitative model. Your job is not simply to agree or disagree. Your job is to decide whether the recommendation is trustworthy enough for leadership to use.

That means you should look for two kinds of problems. First, check the technical side: formulas, constraints, Solver setup, calculations, feasibility, and sensitivity results. Second, check the managerial side: whether the numbers are being interpreted correctly and whether the recommendation follows from the model.

Workflow

Use the Same Seven Steps Every Time

Step 1Read the executive report.
Step 2Verify the quantitative model.
Step 3Correct any errors.
Step 4Classify findings by severity.
Step 5State confidence and uncertainty.
Step 6Write a one-paragraph executive brief.
Step 7Disclose responsible AI use.

Findings

What Counts as an Audit Finding?

Model Structure

A decision variable is missing, the objective function is wrong, a constraint is omitted, or the model does not match the business situation.

Computation

A formula is incorrect, the reported solution does not match the model output, or a calculation is copied incorrectly into the report.

Feasibility

The recommendation violates a stated resource limit, minimum requirement, logical condition, or other business rule.

Interpretation

The math may be correct, but the report misreads slack, binding constraints, shadow prices, allowable ranges, risk, or the practical meaning of the result.

Severity Guide

Classify Findings by Decision Impact

SeverityMeaning
CriticalWould likely lead leadership to make the wrong decision.
MajorSignificantly changes interpretation, feasibility, profitability, risk, or resource use.
MinorShould be corrected but does not change the overall recommendation.

Critical Example

The report recommends a production plan that violates a capacity constraint. If leadership follows it, the plan cannot actually be executed.

Major Example

The report says more labor hours should be requested, but the model shows labor has slack and budget is the binding constraint. The recommendation should change.

Minor Example

A table label is unclear or a rounded value is slightly inconsistent, but the final recommendation remains supported.

Mini-Audit Example

How a Finding Becomes a Recommendation

Suppose an executive report says, "The company should request more labor hours because labor is the limiting resource." When you review the Solver output, you find that the labor-hours constraint has positive slack, while the budget constraint has zero slack.

A strong audit finding would say that the report misidentified the scarce resource. This is a Major finding because it changes the resource request leadership should make. The corrected recommendation would be to evaluate whether additional budget is available, not to request additional labor hours.

Cover Information

Audit Disposition

State whether the original recommendation should be accepted as written, accepted with corrections, revised before use, or rejected.

Findings

Model Evidence

Executive Brief

Write one professional paragraph for leadership. It should avoid equations and spreadsheet language and focus on the decision consequence.

Confidence Statement

State your overall confidence, identify the assumption that gives you the least confidence, and explain what additional information would improve the audit.

AI Disclosure

Report whether you used generative AI, what tool you used if applicable, and how it supported your work. You remain responsible for verifying the final submission.

Executive Brief

How to Write the Leadership Paragraph

Your executive brief should translate your audit into a decision. It should not read like a math solution, and it should not require leadership to inspect your spreadsheet.

Leadership should [accept / revise / reject] the original recommendation because [main finding]. The corrected analysis shows [business consequence]. Therefore, Cowford should [recommended action].

Common Mistakes

Before You Submit, Avoid These Problems

  • Do not only say whether the arithmetic is correct.
  • Do not paste raw Solver output without explaining what it means.
  • Do not call every issue Critical; severity depends on decision impact.
  • Do not write the executive brief like a step-by-step math solution.
  • Do not use AI-generated language without checking the model and reasoning yourself.
