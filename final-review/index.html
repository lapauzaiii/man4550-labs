03 / Model selection

Match the question to the model

Allocation and operations

  • LP: continuous resource allocation with linear objectives and constraints
  • Integer/binary programming: indivisible choices or yes/no investments
  • Transportation/transshipment: flows through a network
  • CPM/PERT: activity precedence and completion uncertainty

Prediction and choice

  • Simple regression: one predictor and a response
  • Multiple regression: several predictors, diagnostics, and confounding
  • Decision analysis: alternatives, uncertain states, payoffs, EMV, regret, EVPI
  • Capital allocation: select a feasible portfolio under budget and risk
Ask the room: Which tool estimates a lease's rent, and which selects among renovation projects?

04 / Optimization

Feasible is not the same as optimal

A solution must satisfy every relevant constraint before its objective value is compared with alternatives. An optimal solution is optimal for the specified model, not automatically for the business.

Challenge: A staffing model maximizes contribution but leaves 50 hours unused. Does this prove the solution is inefficient?
No. The unused hours are slack. Another constraint, such as the budget, may bind. Additional hours alone may not improve the objective. Verify the active constraints and the model's objective.

05 / Sensitivity and integers

What changes when the world changes?

Sensitivity

Interpret binding constraints, slack, shadow prices, and allowable changes only within the assumptions and ranges supporting the analysis.

Discrete decisions

A fractional LP relaxation may provide a bound but not a feasible purchase plan. Binary decisions require whole, permissible choices.

Challenge: Solver recommends 2.4 delivery trucks. What should management do?
Reformulate the truck count as integer. Do not round blindly: rounding may violate constraints or sacrifice the best feasible objective value.

06 / Networks and schedules

Why the shortest-looking path may mislead

Transportation models must satisfy flow balance and lane capacity. Project schedules must respect precedence; the critical path controls the baseline completion time.

Challenge: A project has an expected duration of 18.67 weeks. Is promising completion in 19 weeks a safe commitment?
Not from the mean alone. Examine schedule variance, critical-path uncertainty, the target probability, and consequences of a late delivery. PERT and simulation can produce different approximations because their assumptions differ.

07 / Regression

Prediction is not causation

Coefficients describe conditional associations under a specified model. R², adjusted R², residuals, statistical uncertainty, and multicollinearity help assess whether a model is useful.

Challenge: Adding parking spaces raises R² slightly, but its coefficient becomes unstable and its p-value is high. Must the larger model be preferred?
No. Additional predictors can increase unadjusted R² without improving generalization. Evaluate adjusted R², residual behavior, coefficient stability, collinearity, and the decision's purpose.

08 / Decisions under risk

Expected value is not a guarantee

EMV weights each outcome by its probability. EVPI measures the maximum expected benefit of perfect information under the model. Regret evaluates opportunity loss relative to the best choice in each state.

Challenge: A warehouse option has the highest EMV but a large downside in one state. Is EMV enough to recommend it?
Not necessarily. EMV is a useful benchmark, but risk tolerance, liquidity, downside exposure, probability quality, and implementation constraints can change the recommendation.

09 / Cowford executive challenge

The model is correct. The recommendation is not.

Scenario: Cowford Holdings selects a capital portfolio using a feasible binary model. Its projected value depends on a logistics project finishing on time. Management proposes announcing the full projected return as certain.

In pairs, prepare a 60-second board response: (1) What is numerically defensible? (2) Which assumption deserves scrutiny? (3) What would you recommend before committing?

Separate the optimized portfolio from the certainty of its forecast. Test schedule-dependent payoffs, probability sensitivity, implementation constraints, and downside scenarios. Present a conditional recommendation with monitoring triggers.
